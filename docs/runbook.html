Run It & Call

A step-by-step guide for whoever is actually running this raffle day to day: who runs it, how the prizes and tickets work, and the four-week action list.

01

The permit question is answered: none needed

Checked 21 September 2026. This raffle does not need a minor gaming permit, so there is nothing to wait on before tickets go on sale.

What that does not change. It still runs under Tasmania's minor gaming rules, so run it the way the rules expect: draw it publicly with a second person watching, publish the number of tickets sold before the draw, tell the winners directly, and keep the ticket list. All of that is in this guide anyway, because it is also just the honest way to run a raffle.

Keep the answer with this guide along with the date it was checked. If anyone ever asks, that is the record.

02

Decide who's actually running it

  • If Step 1 says an eligible organisation is needed: line one up now — a local service club, sports club, church group, or registered charity willing to auspice the raffle. Usually a quick yes once you explain it's for a well-known local man's van.
  • Either way, decide and write down before launch: who holds the cash day to day (one named person, one place), how the money reaches Chris at the end, and who signs off the final winner list and the total raised.
03

Line up the prizes into four packs

Don't hand out the gift cards as one big pile. Group the roughly $1,000 into four named packs so the raffle has four winning moments instead of one.

PackRoughlyWhat goes in it
The Big One~$400The highest-value cards, combined
Ulverstone Local Pack~$300Mid-tier cards from 3–4 different local businesses
Night Out Pack~$200Cafe / takeaway / entertainment cards
Early Bird Draw~$100One smaller card, drawn a week before the main event

Photograph each pack once assembled — Bridie needs these photos for the prize board design.

04

Set the price and the two dates

  • Ticket price: $10 single, $25 for 3, $45 for 6. Round dollars only.
  • No cap on tickets. Sell on a rolling basis until the draw date — the prize cost is fixed, so uncapped sales maximises cash for Chris. State on every ticket that total tickets sold and odds will be posted the morning of the draw.
  • Early bird cutoff: a date roughly 10–12 days into the campaign.
  • Draw day: one fixed date about four weeks out, ideally at a real local event with a crowd already there — the Ulverstone market, a footy final, show day, whatever's on the calendar.
05

Four-week run sheet

WEEK 1

Set up & launch — prize packs assembled and photographed, ticket book printed, hero post and ticket graphic live, ticket books dropped at donating businesses' counters.

WEEK 2

Push to the early bird cutoff — daily-ish posts in local Facebook groups, ask each donating business to share, announce the early bird winner the day the cutoff hits.

WEEK 3

Keep the pace up — share a running tally ("$X raised so far"), reshare the early bird winner's reaction if they'll let you, confirm the draw-day venue and who's pulling the tickets.

WEEK 4

Draw day — post total tickets sold and total raised before the draw starts, draw live on video in front of a crowd if possible, same-day thank-you post naming every donating business.

06

Draw day checklist

  • Total tickets sold counted and written down
  • Total raised written down
  • All four prize packs on hand and visible
  • Someone filming or photographing the draw
  • A second person witnessing the draw, named and written down
  • Winners' names and phone numbers ready to record as each ticket is pulled
  • Thank-you post drafted in advance, just needs the winners' names dropped in
